NPI Credentialing & Payer Enrollment Roadmap.

The operational path from application to direct payer billing. Credentialing is what converts InPursuit Health from an app company into a digital clinical services provider — the company's primary revenue engine (Engine 02, the Partner Network). The consumer app (Engine 01) generates first revenue at the mid-July 2026 launch; this roadmap is not about revenue in the next few weeks. It is the build plan for the healthcare company that executes from Q4 forward.

Status · June 11, 2026

No application has been filed. NPI credentialing, Medicare enrollment, and commercial payer credentialing are funded by round proceeds and commence at round close. All timelines below are stated as T+ weeks from close. Managed correctly, total elapsed time from initiation to full multi-payer billing capability is 90–120 days — landing direct billing in Q4 2026, the planned revenue inflection point.

Why This Matters

InPursuit Health, LLC bills Medicare and commercial payers directly under its own Type 2 NPI — not through partner practices, and never as a percentage of any practice's payments. Without enrollment, the company cannot bill any payer for RPM, CCM, TCM, BHI, or APCM (Engine 02); cannot credential with commercial payers, most of whom require active Medicare enrollment first; cannot apply as a direct ACCESS Model Participant under its own TIN for Cohort 2; and cannot operate the zero-cost partner model, which depends on InPursuit earning from payers rather than charging practices. Part of the $750K Phase 1 clinical stand-up allocation in the round use-of-proceeds funds this entire sequence.

The Three Gates
Gate 1 — Type 2 NPIT+1 wk file · 1–10 days to issue

Organizational National Provider Identifier via NPPES. A 20-minute online application; free; typically issued within 1–10 business days. Requires the LLC's EIN/TIN, registered business address, Authorized Official (CEO), and provider taxonomy code. This is the prerequisite for everything else — Medicare enrollment is rejected without it and every commercial application requires it. Files in week one of Phase 1.

Gate 2 — Medicare Part B Enrollment (CMS-855B)T+4 wks file · 30–90 days to process

Filed through PECOS once the NPI is active. 2026 application fee: $750. CMS conducts primary-source verification of ownership, licensure, and compliance; a site visit may be required; development letters must be answered within 30 days or the application is withdrawn. Requires 5%+ ownership disclosure (InPursuit Partners, LLC), an organizational structure chart, a contracted supervising physician (Medical Director), professional liability insurance, EFT authorization, and Tennessee state requirements for the initial market.

Gate 3 — Commercial Payer CredentialingT+2 wks start · 45–120 days · runs parallel

Begins as soon as the NPI is active, in parallel with Gate 2, via CAQH ProView (attestation renews every 120 days — a lapsed attestation rejects all pending applications). Initial Tennessee market panel: UnitedHealthcare (30–120 days), BCBS of Tennessee (60–120), Aetna (60–90), Cigna (45–90, often fastest), Humana (60–90, key for Medicare Advantage). All applications submit simultaneously; payers that require active Medicare enrollment complete after Gate 2 closes.

Parallel Requirements

Medical Director. Contracted licensed physician for clinical oversight and supervisory authority, compensated at fair market value for services rendered. Required for Medicare enrollment and care-management orders.

Professional liability insurance. Required by Medicare and all commercial payers before any application is submitted. $20–40K Year 1 for a care-management organization.

Compliance program. Written policies, compliance officer, training, reporting. The SOC 2 and HITRUST e1 work already in the round use-of-proceeds satisfies the majority of requirements.

Clinical protocols & documentation. Standardized care plans, escalation protocols, and payer-compliant documentation workflows for each billable code (RPM, CCM, TCM, BHI, APCM).

RCM infrastructure. Claims submission, clearinghouse enrollment, eligibility verification, denial management, AR follow-up. Outsourced to an RCM partner initially.

Care-management staffing. 1 care coordinator per 200–300 RPM/CCM patients; 1 supervising RN per 3–5 coordinators. Anchor-practice launch: 2–3 coordinators plus 1 RN supervisor.
