InPursuit Health, LLC · Class C Offering

Exhibit C

Offering disclosures and risk factors · Class C non-voting economic membership units

These disclosures are provided to every prospective investor in the Class C Units of InPursuit Health, LLC. An investment in the units involves a high degree of risk and is suitable only for investors who can bear the complete loss of their investment. Read this document in full before subscribing.

A. Purpose; no offer

This document accompanies the Company's investor data room materials and the Subscription Agreement for the Class C Units. It does not constitute an offer to sell or a solicitation of an offer to buy securities in any jurisdiction where such offer or solicitation would be unlawful. The Units are offered only to accredited investors through definitive subscription documents. Nothing in the data room, the Company's website, or any presentation is a substitute for the Operating Agreement and the Subscription Agreement, which control.

B. Confidentiality

The data room materials, including financial information, partner information, product roadmaps, and these disclosures, are confidential. By accessing them, each prospective investor agrees not to reproduce or distribute them, in whole or in part, or to use them for any purpose other than evaluating an investment in the Company.

C. Non-registration; restricted securities

The Units have not been and will not be registered under the Securities Act of 1933 or any state securities laws. They are offered in reliance on exemptions from registration available for offers and sales to accredited investors. The Units are restricted securities: they may not be resold or transferred except in compliance with the Operating Agreement, applicable securities laws, and any transfer restrictions in the Class C unit designation. No public market exists for the Units, and none is expected to develop. Investors must be prepared to hold the Units indefinitely.

D. Risk factors

D.1 Early-stage company

The Company is an early-stage company with a limited operating history, limited revenue history, and significant ongoing capital needs. The Company's success depends on execution against its product, partnership, and regulatory strategies, none of which is assured. The Company may require additional capital beyond this offering, and such capital may not be available on acceptable terms or at all.

D.2 Illiquidity; indefinite hold

There is no market for the Units and no right to require the Company to repurchase them. An investor may be unable to liquidate this investment for an indefinite period, including through and beyond any future corporate conversion or financing.

D.3 Non-voting interests

Class C Units carry no voting rights. Holders will have no ability to influence management, the Board of Managers, or the outcome of matters submitted to voting members, including amendments, financings, and sale transactions, except to the limited extent required by non-waivable provisions of Wyoming law.

D.4 Distributions not guaranteed

The Company has no obligation to make distributions and does not expect to make distributions for the foreseeable future. Cash generated by the business is expected to be reinvested in operations.

D.5 Dilution; oversubscription; advisory pool

The Board has authorized the acceptance of oversubscriptions in this offering up to $5,000,000 in the aggregate at identical terms. Acceptance of oversubscriptions, together with future issuances of equity, profits interests, or other securities, will dilute the percentage interest represented by the Units. Future financings may carry rights senior to the Class C Units.

Advisory pool. The Company maintains an advisory membership pool of up to approximately 5% of the fully-diluted capitalization, issued as Class C-1 non-voting profits interests that are junior and subordinate to the Class C Units and subject to vesting and forfeiture. The pool is included in the pre-money fully-diluted base for this offering, so that its dilution is borne by the existing members and does not reduce a Class C investor's negotiated percentage for this offering. Issuance of units from the pool, and the vesting of previously granted pool units, will dilute the aggregate percentage interests of all members, including holders of the Units.

D.6 Waterfall position; existing preferred

The Company's existing Class B units carry a preferred return under their offering documents and rank senior to the Class C Units. In the Company's distribution waterfall, distributions are made first to the Class B units to the extent of their preferred entitlement, then to the Class C units, then to the Class C-1 advisory units, with the Class A units participating as provided in the Operating Agreement. The Class C units are therefore subordinate to the Class B units and senior to the Class C-1 units. Investors should review these provisions carefully before subscribing.

D.7 Possible future reorganization

The Company is a limited liability company and intends to remain one. The founder may elect to reorganize the Company into a different entity or tax structure at a later date. Upon any such reorganization, the Units would be exchanged for the corresponding equity of the successor entity under the Operating Agreement. The form, class, and terms of the equity received would be established at that time, and a reorganization may change the legal form, tax treatment, and rights associated with an investor's holding. There is no assurance that any reorganization will occur, or as to its timing or terms.

D.8 Valuation

The $20,000,000 pre-money valuation was established by the Company and is not the product of an independent appraisal, an arm's-length negotiation with an institutional lead investor, or a trading market. It may not reflect the price at which the Units, or successor securities, could actually be sold.

D.9 Reliance on management; conflicts

Investors will be relying entirely on the Company's management team. Members of management have other business interests and hold interests senior or structurally different from the Class C Units. The Company's counsel represents the Company, not the investors; each investor must engage its own advisors.

D.10 Healthcare regulatory environment

The Company operates in a heavily regulated industry. Its business depends on, among other things, federal and state healthcare regulation, payer policy including Medicare rulemaking, data privacy and security law, and fraud and abuse law. Changes in law, regulation, or payer policy, or any failure to maintain compliance, could materially and adversely affect the business.

E. Tax disclosures

The Company is a limited liability company. Investors admitted as members will receive an annual Schedule K-1 and may be allocated taxable income without receiving corresponding cash distributions ("phantom income"). Each investor is responsible for its own tax obligations arising from the investment. The founder may elect to reorganize the Company into a different entity or tax structure at a later date, which could change the tax treatment of an investor's holding.

The Company's federal income tax classification is under review by company counsel, and no representation is made regarding the Company's classification or the availability of any particular tax treatment. Any potential benefits under Sections 1202 (qualified small business stock) or 1244 of the Internal Revenue Code would apply, if at all, only to equity of a qualifying corporation following a future reorganization, only if statutory eligibility requirements are met at that time, and with holding periods commencing only at that reorganization. No tax outcome is guaranteed. Investors must consult their own tax advisors.
