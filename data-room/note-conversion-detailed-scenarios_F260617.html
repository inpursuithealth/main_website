01 — HOW TO READ THESE SCENARIOS

Three levers drive every outcome.

Your note locks in at a $20M post-money cap. What that's worth at the Series A depends on three things — all of which follow from simple arithmetic, not from any forecast of the company's value.

LEVER 1

Your ownership floor

Set the day you sign and grown by interest. Principal plus 5% accrued interest, divided by the post-money cap.

(Principal + interest) ÷ $20M
LEVER 2

The cap advantage

Your paper markup at conversion — how far the Series A prices above the $20M you locked in.

Series A pre-money ÷ $20M
LEVER 3

Dilution

New Series A money and any option-pool top-up dilute every holder, including you.

04 — SCENARIO BY SCENARIO

The same Note, four endings.

Scenario A · At the cap1.0×

The Series A prices at the $20M cap.

Your note converts at the same price new investors pay. No markup, no penalty — principal plus accrued interest convert into Series A preferred at your ownership floor. A $50K note holds ≈ 0.27% and a $100K note ≈ 0.54% at the 18-month interest ceiling, each subject to dilution from the new round.

This is the floor case for an at-or-above-cap round. You are now a preferred shareholder alongside the Series A.

Scenario B · Step-up2.0×

The Series A prices at roughly twice the cap.

New investors come in at the higher round price; you keep the $20M cap. That spread is the early-investor advantage the note exists to capture — and it applies to your accrued interest as well as your principal, marking your conversion basis up about 2× before dilution.

This is the range a disciplined Series A is most often defended in, given the cap-to-raise ratio. It remains illustrative, not a forecast.

Scenario C · Strong round3.0×

The Series A prices well above the cap.

The same mechanics, amplified: the further the Series A prices above $20M, the larger the markup your locked-in cap captures on both principal and interest relative to new money entering the round.

Upside is unbounded by the cap on the high side — but it is paper value in an illiquid security until a liquidity event, and dilution still applies.

Scenario D · Maturity / downsideBackstop

The priced round never happens.

Here is where the note differs from a SAFE. If no qualifying Equity Financing closes within 18 months, the note matures: principal plus accrued interest becomes due as a repayment obligation. In a wind-down you rank as a creditor — ahead of SAFE holders and equity — a senior claim a SAFE does not carry. Recovery may still be partial or zero, and if the debt becomes worthless a §166 bad-debt deduction may apply.

This is the central risk of early-stage investing: you can still lose your entire investment. The note's seniority and maturity improve your position relative to equity — they do not eliminate the risk.

05 — TWO THINGS WORTH KNOWING

Below the cap, and the debt backstop.

If the round prices below $20M

You convert lower — and get more

With no discount and cap-only terms, a Series A priced under $20M means you convert at that lower round price, receiving a larger ownership stake than the cap floor implies — on principal and accrued interest alike.

IRC §166 — Bad-debt deduction

A path only debt holders have

Because the note is bona fide debt, if it becomes wholly or partially worthless you may be able to claim an ordinary-loss bad-debt deduction — available to debt holders without waiting for a C-Corp conversion. Eligibility depends on a worthlessness determination and your circumstances — consult your tax advisor.

If maximum simplicity matters to you

The bridge is also offered as a SAFE — same $20M cap and conversion mechanics, but with no interest, no maturity, and no repayment obligation: the simplest possible terms. The Note adds debt seniority over the SAFE in a wind-down, 5% accruing interest that increases conversion shares, an 18-month maturity backstop, and a §166 bad-debt path. Same upside basis, more protection. Ask for the SAFE brief to compare side by side.
