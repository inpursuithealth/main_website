01 — WHAT A CONVERTIBLE NOTE IS

A short-term loan that becomes equity — with a backstop either way.

A convertible note is a loan you make to InPursuit today that is designed to convert into equity at a future financing — InPursuit's Series A. Unlike a SAFE, the note is debt: it carries interest and a maturity date, which means you hold a repayment claim if the priced round never closes.

In one sentence

You lend today at a $20M post-money valuation cap. When the Series A closes, your principal plus accrued interest automatically converts into preferred equity priced at that cap — regardless of how high the Series A is valued.

Interest accrues at 5% per year. If no qualifying financing closes by the 18-month maturity, the note comes due as a repayment obligation rather than simply waiting.

02 — WHAT TRIGGERS CONVERSION

The Series A is the conversion event.

Your note converts automatically upon the closing of a qualified Equity Financing — defined as a priced equity round raising at least $1,000,000 in gross proceeds. InPursuit's Series A is targeted at $13M, closing in Q4 2026.

When that round closes, conversion is automatic — it requires no action on your part. If no qualifying financing closes within 18 months, the note matures: principal plus accrued interest becomes due, giving you a creditor's claim a SAFE does not carry.

03 — HOW CONVERSION WORKS

Four steps, fully automatic.

1

Series A closes

A qualified equity financing of $1M+ triggers the conversion. Target: $13M, Q4 2026.

2

Conversion price is set at the cap

Your conversion price is the $20M post-money cap divided by the company's capitalization — not the (higher) Series A price. The cap alone governs.

3

Principal + accrued interest converts to preferred equity

(Principal + 5% accrued interest) ÷ Conversion Price = the equity you receive, issued as Note Preferred — the same class and series as Series A investors. The accrued interest converts into additional shares.

4

You hold Series A preferred stock

Upon InPursuit's planned C-Corp conversion at the Series A, your preferred units become preferred stock, with the rights of that round.

You enter early. You're priced early.

What else the Note carries.

07 — TAX TREATMENT

Three provisions — one available now.

A convertible note is debt, which gives it a downside provision a SAFE never has. §166 attaches to the debt itself; the other two activate once the note converts to stock and InPursuit completes its planned C-Corp conversion at the Series A.

IRC §166 — Bad Debt

Available now

Because the note is bona fide debt, if it becomes wholly or partially worthless you may be able to claim an ordinary-loss bad-debt deduction — a path open to debt holders that SAFE holders never have.

Available to debt holders
IRC §1202 — QSBS

Capital-gains exclusion

On conversion to qualifying C-Corp stock held five-plus years, gains may be eligible for exclusion of up to 100% (capped at the greater of $10M or 10× basis), subject to the statute's requirements.

Upon C-Corp conversion
IRC §1244 — Ordinary Loss

Downside protection

If the investment results in a loss after conversion, §1244 may allow up to $50,000 ($100,000 for joint filers) to be deducted as an ordinary loss rather than a capital loss.

Upon C-Corp conversion
